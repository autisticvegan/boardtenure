Heads I win, tails you lose

Paying executives in stock is meant to make them feel what shareholders feel. Since 2022 the SEC has required companies to report “compensation actually paid”: reported pay with every stock award re-valued at the year-end price, so it falls when the stock falls.

At the 1,267 companies whose shareholders lost money over the table's years, the CEO's pay actually paid still added up to more than zero at 95% of them. The median CEO kept 80% of reported pay after the re-valuation; the median shareholder kept $48.30 of every $100.

Pay actually paid re-values only awards that were unvested or vested that year, and keeps every dollar of cash. It leaves out shares the CEO already owned, so for executives who own large stakes it understates what they lost on paper. Below: the most pay actually paid where shareholders lost money.
