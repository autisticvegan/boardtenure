The rare exception

Clawed back, forfeited, or left with nothing

Sometimes the parachute doesn't open: pay forfeited on the way out, or money handed back later.

  • Wells Fargo & Company · 2016
    John G. Stumpf

    September 2016: Stumpf and the board agreed he would forfeit all unvested equity, forgo salary during the board's investigation and get no 2016 annual incentive (g233). Wells Fargo's 2017 proxy values the forfeited awards at $41,155,361 (912,739 shares of 2014-2016 Performance Shares and RSRs, at the September 27, 2016 closing price with Performance Shares at target; 'approximately $41 million') (g231, g232). On April 7, 2017 the board 'determined to claw back additional compensation' of 'approximately $28 million (the value at the time of distribution in March 2016 of shares issued to him following vesting of his Performance Share award granted on March 8, 2013)' (g234, April 2017 8-K).

    source
  • Ford Motor Company · 2017
    Mark Fields

    The Committee let him keep the 2017 Time-Based Unit grant and the annual Performance Unit grant, "however, the $2.5 million strategic incentive grant was cancelled."

    source
  • V.F. Corporation · 2022
    Steven E. Rendle

    Under his existing retirement arrangements Mr. Rendle forfeited his fiscal 2023 equity awards and was not eligible for any fiscal 2023 AIP payout (g448).

    source
  • NIKE, Inc. · 2024
    John Donahoe II

    On retirement he forfeited his fiscal 2025 PSP (annual bonus) award and his unvested equity awards except those covered by retirement-eligibility terms (2025 proxy: "Upon his retirement, Mr. Donahoe forfeited his fiscal 2025 PSP award in accordance with its terms, and forfeited outstanding unvested equity awards in accordance with their terms").

    source
  • PayPal Holdings, Inc. · 2023
    Dan Schulman

    Under the retirement agreement he was required to reimburse PayPal $388,000 to offset part of his 2023 personal use of company aircraft (quoted in evidence).

    source
  • Harley-Davidson, Inc. · 2025
    Jochen Zeitz

    He gave up his change-of-control Transition Agreement when he became Senior Advisor (quoted in evidence).

    source
  • Intel · 2022
    George S. Davis

    No exit pay disclosed.

    source

“Estimated” packages come from tables a company files before the exit, such as a merger proxy's golden parachute table; the rest are what filings say was paid. Stock vesting is valued as the filing values it. The $100 figure is the company's own shareholder-return table over a window inside the person's tenure.
